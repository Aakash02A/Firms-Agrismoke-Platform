# 🧠 AgriSmoke AI &bull; Machine Learning & Physics-Informed Dispersion Model

This document outlines the machine learning architecture, feature engineering methodology, physical atmospheric constraints, and benchmark evaluation for the AgriSmoke AI prediction engine.

---

## 1. Problem Formulation

The Indo-Gangetic Plain presents a unique atmospheric transport corridor:
- **Source Region**: Agricultural paddy harvesting in Punjab & Haryana ($30.0^\circ\text{N} - 32.0^\circ\text{N}$, $74.5^\circ\text{E} - 76.5^\circ\text{E}$).
- **Target Receptor Airshed**: Delhi National Capital Region ($28.6^\circ\text{N}$, $77.2^\circ\text{E}$).
- **Transport Bearing**: $\approx 312^\circ$ (North-West vector).
- **Temporal Window**: Post-monsoon Kharif paddy clearing season (mid-October through November).

The objective is to accurately predict the urban ground-level $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ concentration ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$) and convert it into the Indian Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) National Air Quality Index (AQI).

---

## 2. Feature Engineering & Atmospheric Constraints

Rather than relying purely on a black-box model, the training pipeline applies **domain-guided physical feature engineering**:

| Feature Name | Mathematical Definition | Physical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `effective_fires` | $\text{Fires} \times \left(1 - \frac{0.45 \cdot \text{BioDec} + 0.55 \cdot \text{Machines}}{100}\right)$ | Net combustible biomass taking into account in-situ & biological mitigation. |
| `dir_alignment` | $\max(0.05, \cos^{1.8}(\Delta \theta))$ where $\Delta \theta = \|\theta_{\text{wind}} - 312^\circ\|$ | Transport alignment efficiency toward Delhi. Easterly or southerly winds redirect the smoke plume away from Delhi. |
| `inversion_multiplier` | $(800 / H_{\text{PBL}})^{0.75}$ | Nocturnal thermal inversion volume compression. Lower mixing height concentrates particulates near ground level. |
| `wind_coeff` | Piecewise function of wind speed ($u$) | Balances transport speed vs. ventilation dilution. |
| `ventilation_index` | $u \times H_{\text{PBL}}$ ($\text{m}^2/\text{s}$) | Official CPCB/IMD ventilation coefficient indicating dispersion capacity. |

## 3. Candidate Model Benchmarks

A dataset of **6,500 observation vectors** with calibrated stochastic noise was evaluated across four algorithms using an 80/20 train-test split:

| Model Architecture | $R^2$ Score | RMSE ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$) | MAE ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$) | Training Latency |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Gradient Boosting Regressor** 🏆 | **0.9886** | **17.672** | **9.509** | **9.86s** |
| **XGBoost Regressor** | **0.9878** | **18.310** | **9.551** | **2.74s** |
| **Random Forest Regressor** | 0.9834 | 21.381 | 11.037 | 2.68s |
| **Ridge Linear Regression** | 0.6586 | 96.896 | 60.172 | 0.01s |

**Selection**: The `GradientBoostingRegressor` (`n_estimators=180`, `learning_rate=0.08`, `max_depth=5`) was selected for serialization into `ai-ml/models/aqi_dispersion_model.joblib` due to its highest $R^2$ accuracy and lowest root mean square error.

## 4. Feature Importance Rankings

Analysis of Gini impurity reduction across tree splits in the optimal regressor:

1. **`effective_fires`** (41.25%): Primary driver of fine particulate emission mass.
2. **`urban_baseline_aqi`** (28.41%): Internal vehicular, industrial, and construction particulate baseline.
3. **`inversion_multiplier`** (14.12%): Boundary layer trapping multiplier.
4. **`dir_alignment`** (8.92%): Directional vector pointing toward the NCR receptor corridor.
5. **`wind_coeff`** (4.35%): Wind speed transport and dilution efficiency.
6. **`pbl_height`** (1.85%): Vertical mixing height.
7. **`temperature` & `humidity`** (1.10%): Atmospheric density and secondary aerosol condensation.

## 5. Diurnal Cycle Simulation

The 24-hour diurnal forecast curve is generated by applying hourly meteorological variations:
- **Solar Heating Period (13:00 - 16:00 IST)**: Convective mixing deepens the boundary layer ($600\text{m} - 800\text{m}$), dispersing pollutants and reducing $\text{PM}_{2.5}$.
- **Nocturnal Radiative Inversion (01:00 - 06:00 IST)**: Ground cooling traps calm surface air ($150\text{m} - 250\text{m}$), causing evening smoke influxes to accumulate and peak AQI levels near dawn.
